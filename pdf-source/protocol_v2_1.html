HARDCORE RULE For the first 30 days, weigh the foods that are easiest to underestimate: rice, muesli, seeds, oil, chutney and curd. This trains your eye.

Meal-prep shortcut

  1. Pre-portion chicken into 300 g raw packs for 3-4 days.
  2. Cook a rice batch and weigh the final cooked rice into 220 g lunch and 180 g dinner boxes.
  3. Wash/chop 2-3 days of vegetables at once. Aim for at least two colors per day.
  4. Pre-mix a seed jar with equal parts chia, pumpkin and sunflower seed. Weigh 15 g total each morning.
  5. Keep curd/milk in the fridge and log from the package, not memory.
LACTOSE CHECK (NEW) You use lactose-free milk, but 350 g of regular curd a day has ~10-14 g lactose - about one large glass of milk. Curd is usually easier to digest than milk. If curd gives you gas or bloating, split it across more meals or swap part of it for a lactose-free option. Whey concentrate also contains some lactose.

Hunger control

  • If hungry at night but protein is already on target, add low-calorie volume first: vegetables, fruit, plain curd or a measured starchy food rather than automatically adding another whey scoop.
  • If hunger and low energy are persistent for several days AND bodyweight is dropping faster than target, the fix is more calories - not more discipline.
  • Keep highly palatable snack foods out of the default environment during the cut, but no food is morally "bad". Track it if you choose it.

TRAINING-DAY NUTRITION + HYDRATION

Fuel performance without turning supplements into the plan.

Before training

  • Your 12:00 muesli + milk is a reasonable pre-workout meal if it digests comfortably before the 1 PM gym session.
  • If you feel heavy, shift it earlier or reduce fiber/fat in the final hour before lifting. Do not add extra food just because it is "pre-workout".
  • Normal hydration through the morning is enough for most gym sessions.

ORS / electrolytes

  • Do not make ORS an automatic 1 PM ritual for a normal indoor lifting session.
  • It is more useful when sweat losses are clearly high: long badminton, very hot conditions, prolonged exercise, illness-related fluid loss, or when medically advised.
  • For normal days, water plus ordinary meals is the default.

Post-workout

  • You do not need a 30-minute anabolic-window panic. Your normal lunch after training is already protein- and carbohydrate-rich.
  • Keep the chicken/rice/curd/vegetable meal because it is easy for you to repeat. Consistency beats novelty.

Badminton days + rest days NEW

Day typeRule
Normal badminton (under ~90 min)Same calories as the base day. Your usual badminton is already part of the 2,500 kcal estimate. If play is far from a meal, eat a banana from the day's plan before you play.
Long or extra badminton (90+ min, or more than your usual week)ADD ~150-200 kcal of carbs that day, for example 1 banana + 50 g cooked rice. Log it. Use ORS/electrolytes if you sweat heavily or it is hot.
Gym rest daySame base day. Same calories every day keeps the plan simple. The 7-day average does the rest.

SUPPLEMENTS

Keep the useful, remove the risky or unnecessary.

SupplementStatusRule
Whey proteinKEEPUse to hit the protein target. It is convenience, not a separate category of nutrition.
Creatine monohydrateADD / OPTIONAL3-5 g daily is a well-studied maintenance approach. Timing is not important; daily consistency is. Expect ~1-2 kg of water weight in the first 2-4 weeks. This is not fat. Use rule #2 on page 6 during those weeks.
Fish oilCONDITIONALReasonable if fatty-fish intake is low. Avoid megadosing; check EPA+DHA on the label and discuss with a clinician if you use blood-thinning medication or have bleeding concerns.
Zinc 50 mgCHECK / DO NOT ROUTINELY MEGADOSEIf the tablet contains 50 mg ELEMENTAL zinc, that exceeds the adult UL of 40 mg/day from all sources. Long-term high zinc can also cause copper deficiency. Do not take it nightly long-term without clinician guidance.
MagnesiumCHECK LABELLook at ELEMENTAL magnesium. NIH lists 350 mg/day as the adult UL from supplements/medications (food magnesium is not included in that UL). Higher supplemental doses commonly cause GI effects and may need medical guidance.
Vitamin B12CONDITIONALUseful when intake/status warrants it; not required simply because you lift. Your eggs, chicken and dairy already supply B12. If you were prescribed it or have low B12, follow clinician guidance.
Isabgol / psylliumOK IF TOLERATEDCount it as supplemental fiber and do not let it replace vegetables/fruit. Take it with at least one full glass (~250 ml) of water. Keep ~2 hours between isabgol and any medicine.
LABEL CHECK Send the exact zinc and magnesium labels before we lock their doses. The number on the front of the bottle is not always the elemental dose.

When Phase 1 ends

  • Stop the cut when the waist/visual look is lean enough that your next best move is building, not when a body-fat calculator gives a magic number.
  • Hold maintenance for roughly 2-4 weeks while training performance stabilizes.

EVIDENCE + SAFETY NOTES

Targets are individualized from your observed progress; supplement limits and training principles use external guidance.

  • Jager R et al. International Society of Sports Nutrition Position Stand: protein and exercise. J Int Soc Sports Nutr. 2017;14:20. (1.4-2.0 g/kg/day is a broad evidence-based range for exercising people, with higher needs possible during energy restriction.)
  • Morton RW et al. A systematic review, meta-analysis and meta-regression of protein supplementation in resistance training. Br J Sports Med. 2018. PMID: 28698222. (Gains in fat-free mass plateaued at ~1.6 g/kg/day, upper confidence limit ~2.2 g/kg/day. 175 g is ~2.1 g/kg for you, so more is not needed.)
  • Antonio J et al. Common questions and misconceptions about creatine supplementation. J Int Soc Sports Nutr. 2021. (3-5 g/day is an effective daily maintenance strategy. Creatine can increase body water in the short term, which shows up on the scale.)
  • NIH Office of Dietary Supplements. Zinc - Health Professional Fact Sheet. Adult tolerable upper intake level: 40 mg/day from all sources unless medically supervised.
  • NIH Office of Dietary Supplements. Magnesium - Health Professional Fact Sheet. Adult UL from supplements/medications: 350 mg/day; food magnesium is excluded from this UL.
  • Step energy estimate: walking costs roughly 0.5 kcal per kg per km above rest. At 83 kg and ~1,300 steps/km, 3,000 extra steps is ~100-150 kcal.
HEALTH NOTE This is a fitness/nutrition plan for a generally healthy adult, not medical treatment. If you have kidney, liver, cardiovascular, gastrointestinal or metabolic disease, take prescription medication, or develop concerning symptoms, have a clinician review the plan and supplements.
